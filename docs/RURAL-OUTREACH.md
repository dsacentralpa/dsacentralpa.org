# Rural outreach plan — Clearfield County

## What we're working with

| | Clearfield County |
|---|---|
| Population | ~78,100 (2026 projection) |
| Median age | 45.4 — meaningfully older than PA (~41) or the US (~39) |
| Character | Rural; 36th of Pennsylvania's 67 counties by population |

On connectivity, the county-specific numbers aren't published, but the Center for Rural
Pennsylvania's statewide findings set the expectation:

- About **15% of rural Pennsylvania households have no internet access at all** — roughly 253,000 households.
- **34% of rural residents have cable internet, versus 54% of urban residents.**
- **2.6% of rural residents have fiber, versus 17.1% urban.**
- A statewide speed-test study found **no Pennsylvania county** where at least half the population received service meeting the FCC's broadband definition.

Clearfield County commissioned its own broadband study, which is worth reading before we
finalize anything — it'll have the county-level detail the statewide reports don't.

## What that means for us

**A web-first strategy will miss a large minority of this county.** Not because people are
uninterested — because the pipe isn't there. Three practical consequences:

**1. SMS is disproportionately valuable here.** A text arrives on a flip phone, on one bar,
with no data plan. Email needs a working internet connection and a habit of checking it. For
an older rural population, texting isn't the trendy channel — it's the *accessible* one. This
is the strongest argument for spending money on texting at all, and it's worth making at the
meeting.

**2. Site weight is reach, not aesthetics.** Our page is **under 10 KB, no JavaScript
frameworks, no web fonts, no tracking, no images**. It opens on a bad connection. A typical
Squarespace or Wix page is 2–5 MB and will time out. When someone proposes a prettier site
builder, this is the counter-argument — not "I like code," but "that page won't load in
Karthaus."

**3. Offline has to be a first-class channel, not a fallback.** Which is most of the list below.

## Channels, ranked by likely return

### Tier 1 — do these first

**Get the members-at-large list from national.** Highest-value single action available. There
are dues-paying DSA members in Clearfield County right now receiving nothing local. They've
already self-identified. Comes free with Pre-OC status.

**Paper sign-up sheets that actually collect consent.** Our existing clipboard didn't say
anything about text messages, which is why we can't text those 43 people. Fix the form once
and every future signup is usable. Required wording is in the SMS terms; I'll print sheets.

**The Progress** (Clearfield's newspaper) and local radio. In a county where a third of
households lack decent internet, the paper still functions as infrastructure. Letters to the
editor are free and read. Local radio takes community announcements.

**Physical bulletin boards.** Libraries, laundromats, diners, the courthouse, grocery stores,
union halls. Free, and reaches exactly the people the internet doesn't. A QR code plus a
plain-text URL plus a text-in keyword covers everyone.

### Tier 2 — worth the effort

**Union locals.** Clearfield has building trades, healthcare, public sector, and rail. Existing
organized workers are the least cold audience available to us.

**County fair and community events.** Table with a sign-up sheet. Summer and fall.

**Existing community organizations** — food banks, mutual aid, volunteer fire companies,
churches doing material support. Show up and help before asking for anything.

**Facebook.** Not fashionable, still the town square in rural PA, skews to the age we're
trying to reach. Centre County DSA already runs one; a Clearfield page or group costs nothing.

### Tier 3 — low priority for us

Instagram, TikTok, X. Fine to cross-post, but the demographic fit for a 45-median-age rural
county is poor. Don't spend real time here.

## The one thing I'd emphasize

Every channel above is a way to get someone to a **conversation**, not a signup form. In a
county of 78,000 where the median age is 45 and a third of homes have poor internet, the
thing that actually works is a person you recognize asking you to come to a meeting.

The email and text list is not the organizing. It's the thing that makes sure a person who
already said yes hears about the next meeting. Worth stating plainly at the meeting so nobody
mistakes the tooling for the work.

This also argues for **peer-to-peer texting over broadcast texting** when we get there. A
blast is an announcement; a P2P conversation is an organizer with a phone. Same cost at our
volume — see the budget doc.
